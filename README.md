# Aircraft Washers & Spacers: Aerospace Hardware for Aircraft Assembly and MRO

Aircraft washers and spacers are small but important hardware components used throughout airframe structures, propulsion systems, landing gear, flight controls, avionics installations, and cabin equipment. Washers can distribute fastener loads across a surface, provide a bearing interface, support alignment, or serve specialized locking and positioning functions. Spacers are similarly used to establish controlled distances between components and maintain the intended geometry of an assembly.

In aerospace applications, washers are not simply interchangeable pieces of flat metal. Aircraft-quality washers can be manufactured to specific dimensions, materials, finishes, and performance requirements. ## Functions of Aircraft Washers

One of the primary functions of an aircraft washer is to provide a suitable bearing surface beneath a fastener. By distributing the clamping load over a broader area, a washer can help protect the surrounding material and support the intended fastener installation. Aircraft maintenance references identify plain washers as common hardware used under nuts and in applications involving grip-length adjustment. :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}

Washers can also serve as shims or positioning elements. In some installations, a washer can help establish the required relationship between a bolt, nut, and surrounding structure. Special washer designs can accommodate angled fasteners, internal-wrenching fasteners, or other installation configurations where a conventional flat washer may not provide the required interface.

## Aircraft Spacers for Alignment and Positioning

Aircraft spacers are used to maintain a defined distance between components or establish a specific position within an assembly. They can be found in mechanical equipment, control systems, seating mechanisms, hydraulic equipment, electrical installations, and structural assemblies. Precision spacers can be particularly important where component alignment must remain consistent.

Spacer dimensions may include outside diameter, inside diameter, length, wall thickness, and other geometric characteristics. Depending on the application, the component may be a simple cylindrical spacer, precision-machined spacer, rolled spacer, or another specialized configuration.

## Washer and Spacer Identification for Aircraft Maintenance

During aircraft maintenance, washers and spacers may be identified from part numbers, specification markings, dimensions, drawings, illustrated parts catalogs, maintenance manuals, or other approved technical information. Because several washers can have similar external appearances, dimensional measurement alone may not always establish complete identification.

Important characteristics can include inner diameter, outer diameter, thickness, overall length for spacers, material, finish, locking configuration, and special geometry. The associated fastener can also provide useful context because some washers are specifically designed for particular bolt or screw configurations.

## Aircraft Washers & Spacers in MRO Procurement

Aircraft washers and spacers support thousands of individual assemblies across an aircraft. Their functions can range from distributing loads and protecting surfaces to establishing precise clearances, maintaining alignment, and supporting specialized fastener configurations. Although inexpensive compared with major aircraft components, they remain important elements of properly specified aircraft hardware.

For aviation parts distributors, maintaining accurate information about washer and spacer dimensions, materials, specifications, and application requirements can make procurement more efficient. Customers may require individual replacement pieces, maintenance quantities, production hardware, or specialized components identified by an aircraft or manufacturer part number.

Reliable sourcing depends on treating washers and spacers as specification-controlled aerospace hardware rather than generic items. Matching the correct dimensions, material, finish, configuration, and application helps procurement teams support aircraft maintenance requirements while maintaining consistency with the technical documentation associated with the assembly.
